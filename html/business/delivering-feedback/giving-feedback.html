# Giving Effective Feedback

Turn a vague concern into specific, kind, actionable feedback. Use this skill when asked to give feedback, write a feedback note, prepare to tell someone something hard about their work, or coach a report/peer.

## Choose a Mode

- **Coaching mode (multi-stage):** Use when the user needs to prepare for a live conversation, practice, and plan follow-up. Follow the GAIN-based workflow below.
- **Drafting mode (single-pass):** Use when the user needs a ready-to-deliver feedback note now. Follow the SBI output format below.

## Required Inputs

Ask for these only if they are not already provided:

- **What happened** — the specific situation and the observable behavior (not your conclusion about them).
- **The impact** — what it caused (for the work, the team, the customer, you).
- **Type** — reinforcing (praise worth repeating) or constructive (change needed). Both deserve specificity.
- **The relationship & context** — report, peer, manager; and any relevant history.

## Coaching Mode: GAIN Workflow

1. **Preparation** — Structure the feedback using the GAIN framework:
   - **Goal** — the outcome you want to achieve.
   - **Actions** — the specific, observable behaviors you observed.
   - **Impacts** — the effect of those actions on the work, team, or person.
   - **Next actions** — the specific change or commitment you are asking for.
2. **Roleplay** — Simulate the conversation. Practice delivering the feedback and responding to potential defensiveness. Use a neutral, curious tone.
3. **Refinement** — Review the draft to remove judgmental labels. Ensure the user is 'staying on their side of the net' (speaking only to their own intent and the other person's behavior, not to the other person's intent).
4. **Debrief** — After the conversation, analyze the results. Plan necessary follow-up actions and commitments.

## Drafting Mode: SBI Output Format

### Feedback: [topic] for [who]

**1. The core (SBI)** — the spine of good feedback:
- **Situation** — when/where, specifically ("In yesterday's client review…").
- **Behaviour** — what they did, observable and neutral ("…the demo skipped the pricing slide").
- **Impact** — the effect ("…so the client left unsure what it costs, and emailed to ask").

**2. The ask** — for constructive: the specific change ("next time, walk the pricing slide before Q&A"). For praise: name what to *keep* doing and why it mattered.

**3. Opening line** — how to start so they're ready to hear it (ask permission / state intent: "Can I share something from the review? I want the next one to land even better.").

**4. Make it a dialogue** — 1–2 questions to invite their view ("How did it feel from your side?").

**Calibration note** — keep it timely (soon, not saved for the review), private if constructive, and about the behavior, never the person.

## Core Principles

- **Care personally and challenge directly** (Radical Candor): Balance genuine personal connection with the courage to be direct.
- **Stay on your side of the net**: Acknowledge you only have access to your own intent and the other person's behavior. Speak only to the impact that behavior had on you.
- **Frame feedback as a joint effort**: Position difficult feedback as a collaborative exercise intended to help the person achieve their own goals.
- **Prioritize mutual understanding**: Focus on achieving shared clarity rather than proving who is right or wrong.
- **Separate identity from behavior**: Avoid labeling with character traits. Focus on specific, observable actions.
- **Dial down self-judgment**: Prioritize the needs of the recipient and the core message over your own performance anxiety.
- **Commit to the growth journey**: Reaffirm your commitment to the person's future even when delivering difficult news.

## Quality Checks

- [ ] Built on situation–behaviour–impact (or GAIN), with each part concrete.
- [ ] Behaviour is observable, separated from judgement of character.
- [ ] The requested change (or the keep-doing) is explicit and actionable.
- [ ] It opens in a way that lowers defensiveness.
- [ ] It invites the other person's perspective — a dialogue, not a verdict.
- [ ] Praise is as specific as criticism.

## Anti-Patterns

- [ ] Do not judge character ("you're disorganised") — describe behaviour ("the doc was missing the dates").
- [ ] Do not use the feedback sandwich — burying the point in praise muddles both; be direct and kind.
- [ ] Do not be vague ("be more strategic") — if they can't picture the change, it's not feedback.
- [ ] Do not save it for the review — feedback works when it's timely and low-stakes, not stockpiled.
- [ ] Do not make praise generic ("great job!") — specific praise is what gets the behaviour repeated.

## Based On

GAIN framework (Lenny's Podcast), SBI feedback model (Center for Creative Leadership), and Radical Candor (Kim Scott).
