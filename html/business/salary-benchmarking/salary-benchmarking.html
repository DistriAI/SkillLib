# Salary Benchmarking

Use this skill to build a defensible salary benchmark, either for an individual's market position or for an organizational role/grade register. Choose the track that matches the request.

## Track A: Individual Salary Range

Use when the user asks what they should be paid, whether their salary is fair, or how much to ask for.

1. **Gather inputs.** Ask for the role (title, level, field), location and remote status, the person's profile (years, key skills, notable results), context (current pay, company size/industry, goal), and any sources they have already seen.
2. **Triangulate sources.** Combine salary surveys, aggregators, live job postings, peer/recruiter input, and community data. Do not rely on a single source.
3. **Adjust for drivers.** Apply level/seniority, location/cost-of-living, industry, scarce skills, and company size/stage to the raw range.
4. **Position the person.** Estimate where they sit in the band based on actual experience, skills, and results.
5. **Build a defensible range.** Produce a low/target/high with the reasoning behind each number.
6. **Frame with evidence.** Present the number tied to market data and the person's value; avoid a single cherry-picked figure or an unbacked demand.
7. **Caveat the data.** Note that pay data is noisy and can be stale; triangulate and adjust rather than trusting one source.

### Output Format (Track A)

```
Salary benchmark: [role/level] · [location/remote] · [industry]
Research from: [surveys · aggregators · live postings · peers · recruiters]
Adjust for: level [x] · location/COL [y] · industry · scarce skills · company size/stage
Your position in the band: [where + why]
Defensible range: low [ ] · target [ ] · high [ ] — justified by [factors]
How to frame it: [number + evidence, tied to value]
```

## Track B: Organizational Benchmark Register

Use when the user asks to set up, build, or review a salary benchmarking process for roles, departments, or grades.

1. **Identify intent.** Determine whether the user wants to set up/build artifacts, move an existing process, review/audit, or get advice. Ask only the highest-value missing fact if needed.
2. **Collect missing inputs.** Ask one at a time for roles, market/city/currency, existing bands, current process, and desired outcome. Never invent answers; record unknowns.
3. **Hold internal context.** Maintain a structured record of confirmed facts, open questions, and requested output formats. Do not show this to the user unless asked.
4. **Recommend the smallest workflow.** Benchmark the hardest-to-fill roles first. Store market range beside internal band so the gap is visible. Workflow: Role list → Market data → Internal band → Gap analysis → Band decision.
5. **Build artifacts only on request.** Derive fields from confirmed context and emit the requested formats (CSV, SQL, JSON, Notion, XLSX). For Notion, route to the notion-manual-import skill. For CSV, use UTF-8 with BOM and name columns needing number/date/currency formatting. For SQL, provide DDL with appropriate types.
6. **Safety constraints.** This skill produces empty templates only; it never holds or processes real employee or customer data. Do not use for payroll calculation, tax filing, or legal advice.

### Output Format (Track B)

CSV columns: Role Title, Benchmark ID, Currency, Data Source, Department, Grade Level, Internal Max, Internal Min, Last Updated, Market Max, Market Median, Market Min, Notes.

SQL table: salary_benchmarking with corresponding typed columns.

## Quality Checks
- [ ] Builds the range or register from multiple sources, not one
- [ ] Adjusts for level, location, industry, skills, and company size (Track A) or compares internal vs. market bands (Track B)
- [ ] Positions the person honestly within the band (Track A) or identifies gaps between internal and market (Track B)
- [ ] Produces a defensible low/target/high (Track A) or a benchmark register with min/median/max (Track B)
- [ ] Frames the number with evidence, not entitlement (Track A)
- [ ] Caveats data noise/staleness and triangulation
- [ ] Artifacts are generated only on request and from a single field list (Track B)

## Anti-Patterns
- A single-source number treated as truth.
- Ignoring location/level/industry adjustments.
- Aspirational or imposter-driven self-positioning (Track A).
- A number with no justification.
- Trusting stale data without triangulating.
- Generating artifacts without being asked (Track B).
- Holding or processing real employee data (Track B).
