# Managing Up for Leaders

Transform your relationship with leadership from a reporting line into a high-trust strategic partnership. This skill combines strategic trust-building frameworks with tactical communication planning.

## How to Help

1. **Audit current communication** - Identify if the user is reactive or proactive. Assess their current cadence and how they handle disagreement.
2. **Map the trust building path** - Use the Meat and Potatoes framework to show how executing on core tasks earns the right to pursue bigger swings. Use the Alignment-Confidence Matrix to understand relationship currency.
3. **Structure the operating cadence** - Provide templates for weekly updates that surface blockers and priorities before the manager has to ask. Ensure "no surprises" by flagging risks early.
4. **Script difficult conversations** - Draft specific language for pushing back or asking for resources using business-centered framing. Reframe "pushback" as shifting direction for business success.
5. **Produce a tactical plan** - For specific asks, generate a 5-part plan:
   - **What they need**: Read the manager's pressures and accountabilities.
   - **Frame the ask**: Connect the request to what the manager is measured on, in their preferred format.
   - **Bring vs. escalate**: Clarify what the user will handle vs. what needs the manager's decision. Bring a recommendation, not an open problem.
   - **The message**: Draft a concise message (Slack/email/1:1) that leads with the headline and makes saying yes easy.
   - **Anticipate**: Identify likely pushback and pre-address it.

## Core Principles

- **Reframe self-promotion as education**: Share team value to justify resources.
- **Shift from pushback to business success**: Avoid oppositional language; focus on pivoting toward better outcomes.
- **Prioritize dialogue over being right**: Approach meetings with humility and openness to new perspectives.
- **No surprises**: Keep the manager informed at their preferred altitude.

## Templates & Frameworks

- **The Magic Loop**: Five-step framework for growing career via collaborative manager relationship.
- **Three-Step Process for Saying No**: Listen → Form POV → Respond constructively.
- **Manager Communication Template**: 3-step template for sharing challenges/decisions to earn trust.
- **Alignment-Confidence Matrix**: Framework for understanding relationship currency.
- **Meat and Potatoes Before Soufflé**: Framework for earning trust to pursue ambitious ideas.

## Questions to Help Users

- "What is your current cadence for updating your manager on priorities and blockers?"
- "How do you typically handle a situation where your manager proposes an idea you disagree with?"
- "What is one high-priority task you could take off your manager's plate right now to build trust?"

## Common Mistakes to Flag

- **Reacting with an immediate no**: Creates opposition before listening.
- **Hidden struggle**: Letting teams struggle in isolation.
- **Protagonism trap**: Focusing on personal career narrative over organizational mission.
- **Communicating only when there is a problem**: Creates reactive dynamics and anxiety.
- **Bringing problems without recommendations**: Offloads the manager's job; always bring options + a pick.
- **Mismatched communication style**: Failing to match the manager's preferred detail level or format.

## Quality Checks

- [ ] The ask is framed in terms of what the manager is accountable for.
- [ ] It's in the manager's preferred format and altitude.
- [ ] Problems come with a recommendation and options.
- [ ] It's clear what the user will handle vs. what needs the manager's decision.
- [ ] Likely pushback is anticipated and pre-addressed.
- [ ] The principle of "no surprises" is honoured.
