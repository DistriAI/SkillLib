# Pricing Strategy

## 1. Context & Assessment
- Read any provided files (competitor data, surveys, financials) or project context files (e.g., `contextus.md`, `.agents/product-marketing-context.md`).
- Identify product type, target market, go-to-market motion, and current performance (conversion, ARPU, churn).
- Determine the primary value delivered and the customer's next best alternative.

## 2. Value Metric Selection
- Define the core unit of value that scales with customer success (e.g., seats, usage, transactions).
- Ensure the metric is easy to understand, hard to game, and aligns price with value.
- For variable-cost products (compute, AI), align pricing to cost structure (usage-based or credits) if per-user cost varies widely.

## 3. Pricing Model & Tiers
- Select a model: Flat, Per-seat, Usage-based, Tiered, Freemium, or Hybrid.
- Design 2-4 tiers (Good-Better-Best) with clear differentiation via features, limits, or support.
- Use feature gating based on value, not arbitrary limits.

## 4. Competitive & WTP Analysis
- Map competitor pricing tiers and identify positioning (premium, mid-market, budget).
- Estimate willingness to pay using Van Westendorp or incentive-compatible methods to avoid hypothetical bias.
- Focus on the correct order of magnitude before fine-tuning exact dollar amounts.

## 5. Anchoring & Presentation
- Set the 'Most Popular' tier as the anchor.
- Apply an annual discount (typically 15-25%) to improve cash flow.
- Use decoy effects and clear price displays (monthly vs. annual).

## 6. Iteration & Optimization
- Plan A/B tests for pricing pages and tier names.
- Establish a cadence (every 6-12 months) to revisit pricing as the product matures.
- Monitor signals for price increases (high conversion, low churn, competitor moves).

## Output
- Recommended Model and Value Metric.
- Tier structure table (Price, Segment, Features, Positioning).
- Key assumptions and risks with mitigation strategies.
- Pricing page recommendations (if applicable).
