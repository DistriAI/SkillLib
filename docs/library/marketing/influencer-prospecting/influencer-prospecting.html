The ScrapeCreators copy is a standalone skill that names specific public sources (TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, link-in-bio services, Amazon/TikTok Shop), uses a simpler 1-5 score on six dimensions (niche fit, audience fit, reach, engagement quality, brand safety, contactability), and mandates a CSV export. Crowning one would hide the other's real alternative: the composite's deeper teardown and segmentation versus the standalone's explicit source list and CSV output. They also disagree on whether reach/follower count is a scored dimension (ScrapeCreators scores it; gooseworks explicitly says not follower count alone and does not list reach as a score).

# Influencer Prospecting

Produce a sourced, prioritized creator shortlist that a growth team can actually review and contact.

## Inputs

- Brand, product, target audience, markets, platforms, and campaign objective.
- Exclusions, ideal creator archetypes, follower range, and minimum/maximum shortlist size.

## Workflow

1. Translate the audience and objective into creator search themes, category phrases, hashtags, competitor mentions, and adjacent interests.
2. Discover candidates using public sources: TikTok search users, popular creators, profile, profile videos, and audience demographics; Instagram search profiles, profile, posts, and reels; YouTube search and channel details; link-in-bio services (Linktree, Komi, Pillar, Linkbio, Linkme); Amazon Shop and TikTok Shop showcases where relevant. If the `scrapecreators-api` skill is available, use it for creator search, popular-creator lists, profile search, relevant content, competitor mentions, and category keywords. Fetch each profile plus a recent-post sample.
3. Evaluate public audience and market-fit signals. If the `audience-research` skill is available, use it. Use `creator-profile-teardown`, transcripts, and comments on promising candidates to verify positioning, creative quality, sponsorship style, and audience response.
4. Inspect public link-in-bio pages, business contact paths, creator shops, TikTok Shop showcases, and Amazon Shop pages when relevant. Treat commerce presence as context, not proof of sales.
5. Score each candidate on: audience/niche fit, content fit, product adjacency, reach (followers/subscribers and recent views), engagement quality, consistency, creative quality, sponsorship density, contactability, commerce relevance, and brand safety. Use a 1-5 scale per dimension. Show the evidence behind every score.
6. Check for suspicious engagement, unrelated audience spikes, excessive sponsorship density, category conflicts, and recent safety concerns. Mark unknowns rather than guessing.
7. Segment the final list into test now, nurture, and pass. Match the collaboration idea to the objective: UGC production, gifting, affiliate, sponsorship, ambassador, or paid amplification.

## Output

Return a table with creator, platform, profile link, audience and content evidence, recent median metrics, relevant example posts, public shop or link-in-bio signals, fit score, confidence, risks, public contact path, and recommended collaboration angle. End with a pilot cohort, outreach premise, and test design. Include a CSV block with columns: creator, platform, profile_url, followers, recent_views, fit_score, contact_path, notes.

## Common Pitfalls

- Do not equate large follower count with good fit.
- Do not scrape or expose private contact data. Use public links only.
- Do not hide uncertainty when profiles have limited public data.

Do not scrape private contact data or infer protected attributes. Public business contact details only.
