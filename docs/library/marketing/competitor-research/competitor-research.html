# Competitor Research

## 1. Define Scope and Decision
Identify the specific competitor(s), the research question (e.g., SEO gap, growth history, pricing), and the user's context (stage, budget, product).

## 2. Select Evidence Route
- **SEO/Content Focus:** Use SEO tools (Ahrefs, Semrush) for keyword overlap, backlink gaps, and SERP analysis. Use `site:` operator for indexed pages.
- **General Business/Growth Focus:** Use public web search or supplied materials. Verify claims by reading full passages, not just snippets. Distinguish between public web, supplied files, and connected data.

## 3. Gather Evidence
- **SEO:** Analyze competitor titles, H1s, word counts, H2 structures, and backlink profiles. Identify content and keyword gaps.
- **General:** Record claim, source URL, date, and limitations. Distinguish observed facts from attributed claims. Do not conflate installs with revenue or directory listings with customers.

## 4. Analyze
- **SEO:** Determine length targets, structure adoption, and outreach targets for link gaps.
- **General:** Label conclusions as fact, claim, estimate, or hypothesis. Compare like-for-like metrics. Identify transferable tactics based on user constraints.

## 5. Deliver
- **SEO Report:** Include competitors, research type, findings (gaps/opportunities), and recommendations (content/links).
- **General Brief:** Lead with decision-relevant findings. Include supported timeline, channel evidence, and up to three experiments (observation → hypothesis → test → success measure).

## Conflicts
- **kostja94/marketing-skills** assumes access to paid SEO tools and focuses on technical SEO metrics.
- **davepoon/buildwithclaude** assumes general web access and focuses on verifying business claims and growth narratives without specific SEO tool dependencies.
