# Social Listening Brief

Summarize what people are saying publicly and what the brand should do next.

## When to Use

Use this skill when the user asks what people are saying about a brand, person, product, topic, category, or niche; wants to monitor a brand or category; wants to find complaints or praise; wants a summary of recent social discussion; or wants to compare sentiment across platforms.

## Inputs

- Brand/product, competitors, category terms, market, platforms, and time window.
- The decision this brief should inform.
- Known aliases, misspellings, product names, and campaign phrases.

## Workflow

1. Define the topic, aliases, competitor names, and date window.
2. Build transparent query groups: owned brand, products, competitors, category, problems, and campaign terms.
3. Search multiple relevant public sources rather than every source blindly. Select sources for the audience and category from TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, Facebook, LinkedIn, X, Reddit, Threads, Bluesky, Pinterest, and Google search when platform search is not enough. Use available tools such as scrapecreators-api, WebFetch, or direct platform search.
4. Keep URLs, dates, platform, engagement metrics, and exact quotes for every collected item.
5. Deduplicate reposts and separate owned content, earned mentions, creator content, customer questions, complaints, and spam.
6. Cluster conversations by topic, emotion, purchase stage, and intent. Validate sentiment against the original context; sarcasm defeats naive labels.
7. Separate repeated signals supported across accounts, posts, or platforms from isolated comments and single-post spikes. Compare the current window with an earlier baseline when available.
8. State what was searched but not found; absence in a public sample is not proof that a conversation does not exist.
9. Identify action items for content, community, customer support, creative, landing pages, or product research.

## Output Format

```markdown
# Social Listening Brief: {topic}

## Executive Summary
- Main takeaway:
- Conversation volume: Low/Medium/High
- Sentiment: Positive/Neutral/Negative/Mixed
- Confidence: High/Medium/Low

## Coverage and Limitations
- Query set used
- Sample limitations

## Top Themes
| Theme | Sentiment | Evidence | Representative quote/source |
|---|---|---|---|

## Notable Posts
- [source](url) — why it matters

## Top Questions, Objections, Praise, Complaints, Creator Signals, Competitor Mentions
- ...

## Emerging Risks and Opportunities
- ...

## Recommended Actions
1. Action — owner type — urgency
```

## Common Pitfalls

- Never call a public social sample representative of all customers.
- Do not infer sensitive personal traits.
- Do not pretend this is exhaustive social monitoring. It is public-data research.
- Do not average sentiment across very different communities without caveats.
- Do not use engagement as a proxy for truth.
