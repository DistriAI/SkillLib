# Competitive Analysis

## Inputs
- Your product or company
- Competitors to analyze (3-5 primary)
- Focus: full landscape, feature comparison, pricing, positioning, win/loss
- Audience: product, leadership, sales, board

## Process
1. Identify competitors: direct, indirect, and aspirational benchmarks from adjacent domains.
2. Gather information from provided inputs and available context. Mark assumptions as *(asumido, confirma)*.
3. Build competitor profiles: company overview, target customer, value proposition, strengths/weaknesses, recent activity.
4. Create a feature comparison matrix across dimensions that matter to customers. For each key task, note support level, steps required, UX quality (1-5), and unique approaches. Include notes on quality differences, not just presence/absence.
5. Analyze pricing and positioning. Position competitors on two key dimensions (e.g., Enterprise vs. SMB, Simple vs. Integral). Identify white-space opportunities.
6. Analyze UX dimensions: information architecture, interaction patterns, visual design, content strategy, performance, accessibility, mobile experience. Analyze full journeys, not isolated screens.
7. Identify win/loss patterns and strategic implications by segment.
8. Validate: confirm all claims reference a specific source or are marked as assumptions. Verify feature comparisons note quality differences.

## Output Structure
1. Executive Summary: market position, top 3-5 findings, strategic implications.
2. Competitor Profiles: for each competitor, company overview, target customer, value proposition, strengths/weaknesses, recent activity.
3. Feature Comparison Matrix: table with legend (✅ Complete, ⚠️ Limited/Beta, ❌ None). Include quality notes.
4. Pricing Comparison: table by plan tier.
5. Positioning Map: two-axis positioning with white-space opportunities.
6. Win/Loss Analysis: why we win, why we lose, by segment.
7. Strategic Recommendations: immediate (0-3 months) and medium-term (3-12 months) actions with justification.

## Anti-Patterns
- Do not present competitor feature claims as facts without a source or assumption marker.
- Do not build an analysis that only covers features; include pricing, messaging, go-to-market, and who they hire.
- Do not treat all buyers as identical; win/loss varies by segment.
- Do not soften weaknesses in the analysis to avoid internal discomfort.
- Focus on UX quality, not just feature presence.
- Include aspirational examples from outside the category.
- Update regularly as competitors evolve.
