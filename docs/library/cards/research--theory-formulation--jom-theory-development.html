jom-theory-development

separate

Use when building the theoretical argument and hypotheses for a Journal of Operations Management (JOM) empirical study — deriving operations/behavioral mechanisms a priori, borrowing and adapting reference theory to an OM phenomenon, and specifying mediation/moderation so they are testable against observed operations data.

When to use

  • building theoretical argument for JOM empirical study
  • deriving operations/behavioral mechanisms a priori
  • borrowing and adapting reference theory to an OM phenomenon
  • specifying mediation/moderation for testability against operations data
  • addressing reviewer feedback on correlation vs mechanism
  • matching mechanism engine to evidence type

When not to use

  • purely analytical/optimization work
  • deriving theory as an optimization proof
  • post-hoc hypothesizing (HARKing)
  • importing theory verbatim with no OM-specific adaptation
