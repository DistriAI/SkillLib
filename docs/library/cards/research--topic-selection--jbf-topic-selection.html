jbf-topic-selection

separate

Use when deciding whether an empirical or theoretical finance manuscript fits the Journal of Banking & Finance (JBF) scope, especially banking, financial intermediation, financial institutions, capital markets, investments, corporate finance, or financial regulation.

When to use

  • deciding if a finance paper fits JBF scope
  • evaluating papers on banks, intermediaries, regulation, liquidity, credit, investments, or capital markets
  • determining if a paper is strong enough for JBF or should be routed elsewhere
  • assessing fit for bank capital, liquidity, risk-taking, supervision, or credit supply
  • evaluating financial intermediation, shadow banking, fintech lending, or syndicated loans
  • assessing market liquidity, trading, or asset pricing with institutional frictions
  • evaluating corporate finance questions where banks or financing constraints are central
  • assessing regulation or policy shocks with clear finance mechanisms

When not to use

  • pure accounting, management, macro, or development papers where finance is only context
  • asset-pricing factor papers with no JBF-specific institutional contribution
  • country case studies without credible identification or generalizable finance insight
  • routine event studies without an economic mechanism
  • intermediation theory or structural banking models with tight mechanisms (better for JFI)
  • systemic risk or macroprudential policy evaluation (better for JFS)
  • monetary transmission through banks or macro-banking (better for JMCB)
  • corporate finance where banks are peripheral (better for JCF)
