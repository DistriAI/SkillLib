ahr-historiography-positioning

separate

Use when positioning an American Historical Review (AHR) manuscript within the historiography so it reads as an intervention, not a report. AHR readers are historians across fields who expect you to engage the relevant scholarship and say precisely what you change. Stakes the contribution against prior work; it does not write the literature survey.

When to use

  • Positioning AHR manuscripts within historiography
  • Drafting or revising the introduction
  • Drafting or revising the 'what this changes' passage
  • Engaging relevant scholarship
  • Distinguishing arguments from closest prior interpretations
  • Identifying live interpretive debates
  • Stating the intervention precisely
  • Locating work in historiographical turns

When not to use

  • Writing the literature survey
  • Listing works without an organizing debate
  • Engaging only one's own subfield
  • Using 'first to study' framing for reinterpretation
  • Strawmanning or hiding the closest competing account
  • Treating a recovered topic as self-justifying without a historiographical stake
