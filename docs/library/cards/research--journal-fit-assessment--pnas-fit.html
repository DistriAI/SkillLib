pnas-fit

separate

Use before any writing begins to stress-test whether a result clears PNAS's bar — high quality and broad significance to a general scientific readership — and to make the realistic call between PNAS, Science/Nature, and a specialist field journal.

When to use

  • Deciding if PNAS is the right venue before drafting
  • Judging if PNAS is the right next stop after a Science/Nature desk reject
  • Choosing among Science/Nature, PNAS, and a top field journal
  • Evaluating significance for a general scientific readership
  • Assessing fit for Biological, Physical, or Social Sciences divisions

When not to use

  • Clinical or medical papers with patient outcomes
  • Top-1% general-interest discoveries that are decisive and timely
  • Deep results of interest to only one subfield
  • Rationalizing specialist results into broad significance with adjectives
  • Confusing technical difficulty with significance
