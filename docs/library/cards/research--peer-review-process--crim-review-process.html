crim-review-process

separate

Use to understand how Criminology (ASC / Wiley) evaluates a manuscript — blinded/anonymized peer review, editorial screening, the typical reject / major-revision / minor-revision / accept outcomes, and what expert reviewers weigh in this interdisciplinary, theory-forward field. Sets expectations and shapes the paper to survive review; it does not contact editors.

When to use

  • Stress-test against likely rejection grounds
  • Interpreting a decision letter
  • Understanding what expert reviewers weigh
  • Deciding whether to revise-and-resubmit or send elsewhere
  • Executable review pass
  • Return a ledger of claim, evidence, risk, and location
  • Sibling journal comparison
  • Check for volatile rules in official source map

When not to use

  • Contacting editors
  • Simultaneous submission to another journal
  • Submitting a program-evaluation paper better fit for Criminology & Public Policy
  • Submitting a bare crime correlation with no theory
  • Ignoring measurement validity or the dark figure
  • Expecting acceptance without a revision round
