geb-review-process

separate

Use when you need to understand how Games and Economic Behavior (GEB) actually evaluates a paper — the Editor-in-Charge model, anonymous Advisory Editors and referees, desk-reject and acceptance odds — so you can target the right audience and set expectations. Explains the process; it does not draft content.

When to use

  • understanding who decides on a GEB paper
  • calibrating desk-reject odds and timelines
  • interpreting roles behind a decision
  • writing a cover letter for the Editor in Charge
  • review-risk pass for GEB manuscripts
  • comparing GEB to neighboring outlets like JET

When not to use

  • drafting content
  • naming or guessing referees
  • treating GEB like a double-blind general journal
