cps-theory-building

separate

Use when building or sharpening the theoretical argument of a Comparative Political Studies (CPS) manuscript so it yields portable, testable comparative implications. Builds the mechanism and its observable implications; it does not run the empirics.

When to use

  • Turning a comparative puzzle into a stated argument with implications
  • Addressing reviewer feedback that the paper lacks a theory or is descriptive
  • Addressing reviewer feedback that the mechanism is a black box
  • Creating a portable claim for findings
  • Specifying scope conditions and contrast with rival accounts
  • Building verbal-causal, formal, or comparative-historical theories

When not to use

  • Running the empirics
