comment-mining

combined

Use when the user wants to mine comments and replies for audience reactions, customer language, questions, objections, complaints, product ideas, buying intent, sentiment, or voice-of-customer insights from public social posts and videos. Mine comments and replies on social posts, videos, and ads for recurring customer language, questions, objections, desired outcomes, complaints, product requests, purchase signals, and creative opportunities. Use for voice-of-customer research grounded in linked source evidence.

When to use

  • analyze comments on a TikTok, YouTube video, Instagram Reel, Facebook post, Reddit post, or Rumble video
  • find audience questions, objections, complaints, or buying intent
  • extract voice-of-customer language
  • find content ideas from comments
  • understand sentiment around a post, creator, product, or topic

When not to use

  • flattening comments into generic sentiment
  • including personally identifying details unless public and necessary
  • treating bot/spam comments as audience signal
  • skipping Reddit post context

Conflict

Both copies describe the same core task: collecting public comments/replies, cleaning them, coding them into buckets, clustering themes, and turning findings into marketing/product actions. However, they overlap without one being a clean superset of the other. The gooseworks copy adds a distinct buying-intent strength scale (curiosity, consideration, price/availability, comparison, stated purchase, repeat use, recommendation) and a specific output requirement to report prevalence as sample counts by platform/source type rather than market-wide percentages. The ScrapeCreators copy adds concrete API endpoints per platform, a required environment variable, a structured report template, and explicit pitfalls (e.g., do not skip Reddit post context, call out moderation/platform bias). Crowning one would hide the other's material steps and constraints.
