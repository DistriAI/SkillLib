representations

separate

Use when targeting Representations or deciding whether a new-historicist or interdisciplinary cultural-analysis manuscript fits this venue. Encodes the journal's new-historicism fit, the archive-driven argument bar, material-culture and contextual expectations, house style and double-blind norms, official-submission re-check, and desk-reject heuristics.

When to use

  • new-historicist readings moving between literary texts and non-literary documents
  • material-culture and history-of-the-book studies
  • interdisciplinary cultural analysis across literature, visual art, science, law, religion, and social practice
  • essays organized around a telling particular opened toward a general claim
  • studies of representation itself
  • work in any period or tradition anchored in concrete historical and archival evidence
  • fit/framing check for Representations
  • re-grounding textual readings in material, archival, and historical conditions

When not to use

  • free-floating theoretical essays with no archival or material grounding
  • literary readings sealed off from historical and material conditions
  • theses illustrated by convenient examples rather than argued from specificity
  • gestured-at context with no real reconstruction of documents, practices, or objects
  • wrong venue: a purely theoretical or period-specialist literary venue
