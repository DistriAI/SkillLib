ssc-evidence

separate

Use when handling empirical evidence in a 《中国社会科学》 (Social Sciences in China) manuscript — choosing among quantitative, qualitative, and historical-comparative methods so the method serves the proposition, and translating any analysis (including regressions) into a thesis-level finding rather than a coefficient dump. Use when evidence feels disconnected from the argument or reads as method-flexing.

When to use

  • Choosing quantitative, qualitative, or historical-comparative methods
  • Translating regressions into thesis-level findings
  • Connecting empirical evidence to the core argument
  • Reporting effect magnitudes instead of just significance
  • Refining qualitative materials into concepts or mechanisms
  • Addressing reviewer questions on evidence-argument gaps

When not to use

  • Replacing theoretical argumentation with statistical methods
  • Engaging in methodological arms races
  • Submitting pure empirical work without theoretical propositions to this journal
