comparative-literature

separate

Use when targeting Comparative Literature or deciding whether a cross-national, cross-linguistic, translation, or world-literature manuscript fits this venue. Encodes the journal's comparatist fit, the cross-linguistic argument bar, original-language and translation expectations, house style and double-blind norms, official-submission re-check, and desk-reject heuristics.

When to use

  • cross-linguistic, translation, or world-literature essay fit check
  • re-framing single-tradition readings so comparison is constitutive
  • choosing between Comparative Literature and national-literature or theory-led venues
  • comparison across two or more literatures or languages
  • translation studies and the theory and history of translation
  • reception, circulation, and the cross-border life of texts
  • world literature concepts, methods, and politics of canon
  • comparative poetics, genre, and form across traditions

When not to use

  • single-language reading with a token foreign comparison
  • thematic juxtaposition with no comparatist payoff
  • replacing the journal's current submission guidelines
