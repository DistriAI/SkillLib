scoping-review-guide

separate

Scoping review methodology for broad evidence mapping

When to use

  • map the breadth and nature of research evidence
  • examine the extent, range, and nature of research activity
  • determine whether a full systematic review is warranted
  • identify key concepts, evidence gaps, and types of available evidence
  • map the research landscape before designing a primary study
  • topics too broad or heterogeneous for a systematic review

When not to use

  • answer a specific clinical/research question
  • quantitative synthesis or meta-analysis
  • strictly predefined inclusion criteria
  • required quality assessment or risk of bias
