Use when the user wants to turn public social videos, transcripts, posts, or creator research into reusable content assets such as LinkedIn posts, X threads, short-form scripts, newsletters, blog outlines, carousels, or content calendars. Turn public social videos, transcripts, posts, creator research, or the user's own source material into platform-specific LinkedIn posts, X posts and threads, short-form scripts, newsletters, blog outlines, carousel briefs, and content calendars. Use when the user wants to reuse a strong idea without copying the source.

When to use

  • turn videos or transcripts into posts
  • repurpose TikToks, Reels, Shorts, podcasts, or webinars
  • create LinkedIn posts, X threads, scripts, newsletters, or blog outlines
  • build a content calendar from winning social ideas
  • adapt a competitor/creator pattern without plagiarizing

When not to use

  • plagiarize distinctive language or creative expression
  • flatten every platform into the same format
  • invent facts, personal experience, customer proof, or first-person claims
  • download, transcode, or edit the video
  • introduce an FFmpeg dependency

Skill page

Conflict

The two skills share the same core objective (repurposing source material into platform-specific content) and similar high-level steps (extract atoms, rewrite for voice, avoid plagiarism). However, they differ significantly in execution details and constraints. The gooseworks-ai version is more prescriptive about tooling (specifically forbidding FFmpeg/terminal video processing and mandating specific MCP/CLI paths for transcripts) and includes a detailed 'Runtime paths' section. The ScrapeCreators version is more generic, includes a specific output template structure, and relies on a specific API key environment variable. Neither is a strict subset of the other; gooseworks has better guardrails for media handling, while ScrapeCreators has a clearer output template and different dependency model. Combining them captures the robust media-handling logic of the first and the structured output of the second.
