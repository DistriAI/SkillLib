study-design-identifier

separate

Identifies the real underlying study design used in a medical or biomedical paper, distinguishes primary and secondary design components when papers are hybrid, and converts the paper into an evidence-aware design label suitable for literature appraisal, evidence grading, and downstream review workflows. Always identify the actual design from what the study did, not from how the authors describe it. Never fabricate references, metadata, or study features.

When to use

  • identifying the real study design framework
  • distinguishing primary and secondary design components
  • classifying hybrid multi-layer studies
  • linking design labels to evidence hierarchy
  • grouping papers for literature appraisal
  • checking suspected design labels against methods

When not to use

  • patient-specific treatment recommendations
  • inventing study details from missing methods
  • classifying papers without available content
  • fabricating citations, PMIDs, DOIs, or metadata
