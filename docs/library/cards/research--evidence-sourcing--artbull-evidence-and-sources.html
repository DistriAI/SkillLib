artbull-evidence-and-sources

separate

Use when assembling and handling the non-visual evidence for an Art Bulletin article — objects, archives, primary documents, provenance, and technical findings — and citing them rigorously. Art-historical claims must rest on verifiable sources, not assertion. Guides evidence handling; it does not fabricate documents or attributions.

When to use

  • Building the documentary basis for dating, attribution, patronage, or reception
  • Reconstructing a provenance or ownership history
  • Weighing conflicting documents or an uncertain attribution
  • Handling archival documents, inventories, contracts, wills, correspondence, and sale records
  • Citing technical art history reports such as X-radiography or dendrochronology
  • Distinguishing evidence from inference in art-historical claims

When not to use

  • Fabricating documents or attributions
  • Visual analysis of the object itself
