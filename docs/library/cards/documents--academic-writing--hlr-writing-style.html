hlr-writing-style

separate

Use when shaping the prose and structure of a Harvard Law Review (HLR) piece for a generalist, student-edited audience — clear introductions, disciplined Parts, and the heavy-footnote register of legal scholarship. Polishes how it reads; it does not forge the claim (hlr-thesis-and-contribution) or format citations (hlr-sources-and-bluebook).

When to use

  • Drafting or revising the introduction
  • Revising Part headings or transitions
  • Polishing prose for a generalist audience
  • Correcting text-vs-footnote division of labor
  • Ensuring the thesis is stated early

When not to use

  • Forging the claim
  • Formatting citations
