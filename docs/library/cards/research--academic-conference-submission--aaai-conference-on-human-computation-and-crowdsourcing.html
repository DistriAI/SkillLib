aaai-conference-on-human-computation-and-crowdsourcing

separate

Use when targeting AAAI Conference on Human Computation and Crowdsourcing (HCOMP) or deciding whether a computer-science manuscript fits this venue. Encodes conference fit, framing, evidence bar, submission-cycle checks, rebuttal posture, and desk-reject risks for human computation.

When to use

  • targeting AAAI Conference on Human Computation and Crowdsourcing (HCOMP)
  • deciding whether a computer-science manuscript fits this venue
  • conference fit, framing, evidence bar, submission-cycle checks, rebuttal posture, and desk-reject risks
  • manuscript in crowdsourcing needs a conference-fit read
  • re-framed from journal style or arXiv style into a selective CS conference narrative
  • evidence-gap, anonymity, artifact, rebuttal, or re-routing diagnosis
  • crowdsourcing, human computation, data annotation, human-AI collaboration, and collective intelligence
  • human computation, crowdsourcing, collective intelligence, hybrid human-AI workflows, task design, worker quality, or social/market mechanisms for computation

When not to use

  • substitute for the current year's CFP, author kit, ethics policy, or submission portal
  • generic CS paper until the introduction names the exact subcommunity, contribution type, and proof or empirical standard
  • broader human-centered or social-computing contributions
  • ethics/fairness
  • algorithm rather than human computation is central
  • interactive surface hardware and spatial interaction
  • intelligent interface design
  • general HCI studies when crowd work is not central
