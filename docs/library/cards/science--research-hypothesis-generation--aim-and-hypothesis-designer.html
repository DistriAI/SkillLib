aim-and-hypothesis-designer

separate

Designs primary aims, secondary aims, and testable hypotheses from broad biomedical research ideas. Use this skill when a user needs to convert a loose study idea into a tighter protocol-framing structure with clear aim hierarchy, hypothesis discipline, and separation between hypothesis-driven and exploratory components. Always keep aims answerable, non-overlapping, and aligned to the intended evidence type and study scope.

When to use

  • convert loose study ideas into tighter protocol-framing structure
  • turn broad research ideas into specific aims
  • separate primary study question from supporting questions
  • distinguish confirmatory aims from exploratory analyses
  • write testable hypotheses
  • prevent aim sprawl
  • build protocol-framing layer for study design and analysis planning

When not to use

  • writing patient-specific medical advice or treatment plans
  • fabricating literature support, hypotheses, feasibility claims, or validation status
  • pretending that a full protocol, SAP, or grant narrative has been completed
  • presenting speculative ambitions as already testable without stating missing dependencies
  • generating a long aim list without hierarchy
  • confusing a research topic with a study aim
  • writing decorative hypotheses that cannot be tested
  • hiding major feasibility dependencies
