mathfin-contribution-framing

separate

Use when articulating the contribution of a Mathematical Finance (Wiley) manuscript — frame the methodological novelty and its payoff for financial modelling (pricing, hedging, risk, portfolio, microstructure) so editor and referees see why the theorem matters, not just that it is true.

When to use

  • articulating the contribution of a Mathematical Finance manuscript
  • framing methodological novelty and its payoff for financial modelling
  • reviewing whether the stated contribution matches what the theorems actually deliver
  • addressing cases where the introduction does not say why it advances financial modelling
  • framing the modelling problem, obstruction, and theorem as resolution
  • naming the novelty axis explicitly
  • translating the theorem into a modelling statement
  • bounding the claim to the hypotheses

When not to use

  • routine computation on data
  • claiming practical or empirical impact the paper does not establish
  • selling generality the proofs do not support
  • framing numerical results as the core contribution
  • burying the actual novelty under restated background
