ci-scholarly-positioning

separate

Use to situate a Critical Inquiry (CI) essay within the conversations it enters across the arts and humanities. CI is interdisciplinary, so positioning means naming the critical and theoretical debates the essay joins — across literature, art, film, philosophy, politics, history — and the precise place where it intervenes, not a discipline-internal literature review. Frames the stakes; it does not write the prose.

When to use

  • Defining whose conversation the essay is entering
  • Identifying the specific debate or concept under dispute
  • Locating the exact point where the intervention bites
  • Deciding which interdisciplinary debates to foreground
  • Engaging a specific prior CI piece precisely
  • Mapping the standing view, pressure point, move, and payoff
  • Judging whether the manuscript answers the venue's real reader
  • Returning a claim-evidence-risk ledger

When not to use

  • Writing the prose
  • Conducting a social-science literature review
  • Performing a dutiful survey
  • Listing names without staging a real disagreement
  • Strawmanning the view being overturned
  • Borrowing breadth by citing fields the argument never touches
  • Treating theorists as ornaments rather than positions to argue with
