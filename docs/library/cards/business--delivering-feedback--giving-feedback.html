giving-feedback

combined

Help users deliver high-impact feedback that drives behavior change while maintaining strong relationships and psychological safety through proven frameworks like GAIN and Radical Candor. Turn a vague concern into specific, kind, actionable feedback. Use when asked to give feedback, write a feedback note, prepare to tell someone something hard about their work, or coach a report/peer. Produces ready-to-deliver feedback structured on situation–behaviour–impact, separating observation from judgement, with the change requested and an opening line — calibrated to praise or constructive.

When to use

  • structuring feedback thoughts using the GAIN framework
  • roleplaying feedback conversations to practice responding to defensiveness
  • refining feedback drafts to remove judgmental labels
  • analyzing feedback session results and planning follow-up actions
  • balancing personal care with direct challenge
  • framing feedback as a joint effort for professional goals
  • separating identity from behavior in feedback
  • committing to the employee's growth journey

Skill page

Conflict

The two skills overlap in goal (helping a user give effective, specific, kind feedback) and both reference Radical Candor, but they are not reproductions of one another. The Lenny skill is a multi-stage coaching workflow (Preparation, Roleplay, Refinement, Debrief) built around the GAIN framework and a set of psychological-safety principles, with a roleplay step and a debrief/follow-up step that the other copy lacks. The PM skill is a single-pass drafting tool built around the SBI model, with explicit required inputs, a strict output format (core SBI, the ask, opening line, dialogue questions), quality checks, and anti-patterns (including an explicit ban on the feedback sandwich) that the Lenny copy does not contain. Crowning one would hide a real alternative procedure and framework.
