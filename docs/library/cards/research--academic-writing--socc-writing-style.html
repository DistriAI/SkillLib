socc-writing-style

separate

Use when revising an ACM SoCC paper for a cloud-scale problem an operator recognizes on the first page, a contribution at the systems-and-data intersection, evidence measured on a real or realistic deployment with tail latency and cost as first-class, dual-anonymous wording, and disciplined use of the acmart 12-page budget.

When to use

  • revising an ACM SoCC paper
  • framing cloud-scale problems in operator terms
  • positioning contributions at systems-and-data intersection
  • reporting tail latency and cost metrics
  • pairing claims with measured evidence
  • stating limitations honestly
  • maintaining dual anonymity
  • managing the 12-page budget

When not to use

  • pure-OS results with a cloud title
  • benchmarks with no systems contribution
  • papers reporting only mean metrics
  • papers with untuned baselines
  • papers lacking external validity statements
