# Scientific Writing

## Purpose
Produce clear scientific prose using IMRAD structure, citation-integrated paragraphs, and reporting guidelines. Maintain scientific accuracy and traceability.

## Workflow
1. **Intake & Structure**: Identify document type, study design, and target venue. Select the appropriate reporting guideline (e.g., CONSORT, PRISMA).
2. **Evidence & Provenance**: 
   - Map factual claims to verified evidence IDs.
   - Maintain a source manifest and claim registry to ensure traceability.
   - Do not fabricate citations, data, or results.
3. **Drafting**: 
   - Use a two-stage process: create section outlines, then convert to flowing prose.
   - Write in full paragraphs; avoid bullet points in the final manuscript.
   - Apply the selected citation style (APA, AMA, Vancouver, etc.).
4. **Visuals & Figures**:
   - Write clear captions and in-text callouts for figures and tables.
   - *Conflict Note*: Depending on the specific tooling available, you may be required to generate graphical abstracts and schematics (as per brycewang-stanford) or restricted to writing captions only (as per foryourhealth111-pixel). Check local tool availability before attempting generation.
5. **Review & Submission**: 
   - Check for consistency in units, denominators, and sample sizes.
   - Ensure all declarations (funding, conflicts, AI use) are present and accurate.
   - Verify confidentiality and authorization for any external processing.

## Safety & Ethics
- **No Fabrication**: Never invent citations, data, or authorship details.
- **Confidentiality**: Do not send unpublished or sensitive data to external services without explicit authorization and policy review.
- **Scientific Fidelity**: Preserve uncertainty, distinguish association from causation, and report negative or null findings when appropriate.
