# Scientific Critical Thinking

## Overview

Critical thinking is a systematic process for evaluating scientific rigor. Assess methodology, experimental design, statistical validity, biases, confounding, and evidence quality using GRADE and Cochrane ROB frameworks.

## Appraisal Workflow

1. **Fix the question and unit.** Record population, intervention/exposure, comparator, outcome, time point, effect measure, and target setting. Distinguish descriptive, predictive, and causal claims.
2. **Extract evidence before judging.** Locate the numerical result, uncertainty, denominators, protocol/registration, and analysis plan. Keep missing reporting separate from evidence that a procedure was not performed.
3. **Assess design and analysis.** Check selection, confounding, measurement, attrition, multiplicity, and model assumptions.
4. **Choose the right appraisal framework.** Record the exact tool version and the result being assessed; do not turn checklist counts into a quality score.
5. **Synthesize with scope intact.** Examine independent replication, overlapping samples, and applicability. Apply GRADE per outcome/comparison with explicit domain reasons.
6. **Write a traceable critique.** For each concern give the source location, observation, consequence, uncertainty, and a feasible remedy.

## Core Capabilities

### 1. Methodology Critique
- **Study Design:** Is the design appropriate for the research question? Can it support the causal claims made?
- **Validity Analysis:**
  - **Internal:** Check randomization, confounding control, and selection bias.
  - **External:** Evaluate sample representativeness and ecological validity.
  - **Construct:** Review measurement validation and operational definitions.
  - **Statistical:** Verify power, assumption compliance, and test appropriateness.
- **Control and Blinding:** Assess randomization implementation, blinding feasibility, and control condition appropriateness.

### 2. Bias Detection
- **Cognitive Biases:** Check for confirmation bias, HARKing, and cherry-picking. Verify preregistration.
- **Selection Biases:** Examine sampling, volunteer, attrition, and survivorship biases.
- **Measurement Biases:** Evaluate observer, recall, and social desirability biases.

### 3. Statistical Analysis Evaluation
- Assess power, multiplicity, p-value misuse, and effect sizes.

### 4. Evidence Quality Assessment
- Evaluate study hierarchy, replication, and strength of inference.
