# Peer Review

## 1. Safety and Authorization (Mandatory)
Before reading or analyzing unpublished content:
1. Confirm the user is authorized by the publisher, editor, or author.
2. Check the target venue’s review, confidentiality, and AI/tool policies.
3. Record conflicts of interest and competence limits.
4. Default to local-only processing. Do not send unpublished content to external services or image generators without specific authorization.

## 2. Intake and Scope
1. Establish the submission type, stage, and target venue.
2. Identify available materials (manuscript, supplements, data/code).
3. Record missing material that prevents assessment.

## 3. Initial Assessment
1. Read the abstract and full text to form an overall impression.
2. Identify the research question, claimed contributions, and novelty.
3. Provide a brief summary (2-3 sentences) of the manuscript's essence.

## 4. Detailed Section-by-Section Review
Evaluate each section using these criteria:
- **Abstract/Title:** Accuracy, clarity, and completeness.
- **Introduction:** Context, rationale, novelty, and literature coverage.
- **Methods:** Reproducibility, rigor, ethics, and statistical appropriateness. Verify sample sizes, randomization, and controls.
- **Results:** Logical presentation, statistical reporting (effect sizes, CIs), and objectivity.
- **Discussion:** Interpretation, alignment with results, and acknowledgment of limitations.

## 5. Reporting Guidelines and Evidence Mapping
1. Select the appropriate reporting guideline (e.g., CONSORT, STROBE) for the study design.
2. Map central claims to supporting evidence (figures, tables, citations).
3. Check for alignment between claims and results.

## 6. Issue Organization and Triage
Classify findings into:
- **Major Issues:** Validity threats, methodological flaws, unsupported claims, or ethical gaps. For each, provide: Why it matters, Suggested fix, and Expected impact.
- **Minor Issues:** Clarity, formatting, citations, or language improvements. For each, provide: Suggested fix.

## 7. Recommendation
1. Provide a decision (Accept / Minor Revision / Major Revision / Reject).
2. Explain the rationale based on novelty, rigor, and evidence strength.
3. List the top 3-5 changes that would most improve the manuscript.

## 8. Visual Enhancement (Optional/Authorized)
If the venue policy and authorization permit, and the user requests it:
- Generate scientific schematics to visualize workflows, decision trees, or complex concepts.
- Ensure diagrams are publication-quality and accessible.
