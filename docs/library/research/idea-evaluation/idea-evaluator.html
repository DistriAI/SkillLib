# Idea Evaluator

## Overview

This skill evaluates a preliminary idea from the perspective of a rigorous
reviewer or an impartial judge. It supports two evaluation modes:

1. **Research Mode:** Scores the idea against a five-dimension framework
   (Higher, Faster, Stronger, Cheaper, Broader), runs a fatal-flaws audit,
   checks lifecycle and capability matching, and returns a reviewer-style
   verdict.
2. **Debate Mode:** Orchestrates a three-turn debate between a Pro agent and a
   Con agent, then delivers a business-style verdict.

The goal is to kill weak ideas early and to shape promising ideas into stronger
forms before significant investment begins.

## When to Use This Skill

- The user has a draft idea and asks whether it is worth pursuing.
- The user asks for a novelty check, feasibility assessment, or scoring.
- The user is comparing candidate ideas and needs a structured trade-off.
- The user wants to stress-test an assumption before committing to code or a
  paper scope.

## When NOT to Use This Skill

- The user has already implemented the idea and is writing the paper or
  product documentation.
- The user explicitly wants brainstorming of new ideas from scratch.
- The user asks for review of an existing manuscript or codebase.

## Core Procedure

### Step 1: Classify the Idea and Select Mode

Read the user's idea description. In one paragraph, state whether the idea
reads as a Novel Problem, Novel Method, or New Setting. Classify the research
paradigm by method, not by department name:

- **STEM:** Experiments, benchmarks, ablations, or model architectures. Use
  **Research Mode**.
- **Humanities:** Text analysis, archives, or conceptual argument. Use
  **Research Mode** with substitute frameworks.
- **Empirical Social Science:** Surveys, interviews, statistics, or fieldwork.
  Use **Research Mode** with substitute frameworks.
- **Finance/Economics:** Regressions, IV, DID, RDD, or panel data. Use
  **Research Mode** with substitute frameworks.
- **Law:** Statutes, cases, or doctrine. Use **Research Mode** with substitute
  frameworks.
- **General/Business/Product:** Features, apps, or projects without a specific
  research paradigm. Use **Debate Mode**.

If the paradigm is unclear, ask one question: "Is the core method experiments,
surveys, text analysis, theoretical derivation, or product development?"

### Step 2A: Research Mode – Fatal-Flaws Audit (Early Gate)

Run the fatal-flaws audit before scoring. Identify at most two fatal flaws.
For each, state the flaw, cite the detection rule, and recommend a concrete
defense.

**Ground the novelty flaw in real retrieval whenever possible.** Extract two
or three keyword groups from the idea, search, and name the three to five
closest published works with title, authors, and year. For each, state which
axis actually differs. A similar title alone never establishes duplication.

**A data-refuted core mechanism is an automatic CRITICAL.** If the user's own
reported data already show the core mechanism matched or beaten by a baseline,
lock the verdict to Reject and Pivot.

**Short-circuit rule.** If any fatal flaw is tagged CRITICAL, stop here and
emit the verdict directly: Reject and Pivot. Do not run the five-dimension
scoring, paradigm-shift probe, feasibility check, or integrity gate.

If no CRITICAL flaw is found, continue to Step 3A.

### Step 3A: Research Mode – Five-Dimension Scoring and Lifecycle Matching

Score the idea against the five improvement dimensions: Higher, Faster,
Stronger, Cheaper, Broader. Match the idea's lifecycle against the user's
actual capability and available hours per week. Probe whether the idea has
paradigm-shift potential.

### Step 4A: Research Mode – Verdict

Return one of three verdicts: Strong Accept, Accept with Revisions, or Reject
and Pivot. Provide a rationale grounded in the scoring and fatal-flaws audit.

### Step 2B: Debate Mode – Initialize the Debate

Assume the role of the Orchestrator. Introduce the debate and clearly state
the idea being evaluated. Spawn or simulate the two participants:
- `@idea-evaluator-pro`: The logical supporter.
- `@idea-evaluator-con`: The constructive critic.

### Step 3B: Debate Mode – The Debate (3 Turns)

Conduct a 3-turn debate where the Pro and Con agents respond to each other.
- **Turn 1 (Initial Pitches):** Pro presents the strongest case for the idea.
  Con presents the strongest immediate risks and flaws.
- **Turn 2 (Rebuttals):** Pro addresses Con's risks. Con challenges Pro's
  optimism.
- **Turn 3 (Closing Statements):** Both agents summarize their final stance on
  why the idea will succeed or fail.

Ensure the agents do not blindly agree/disagree but base their arguments on
logic, market realities, and technical feasibility.

### Step 4B: Debate Mode – Final Verdict

Once the debate concludes, the Orchestrator steps in as the Judge. Provide a
comprehensive summary formatted with:
- **Pros:** The strongest validated points in favor.
- **Cons:** The most critical risks identified.
- **Final Verdict:** A definitive recommendation (e.g., "Strongly
  Recommended", "Proceed with Caution", "Pivot Required", "Not Worth
  Building").
- **Why:** A brief justification summarizing the debate outcome.

## Best Practices

- Ensure the Pro and Con agents directly address each other's points during
  rebuttals.
- The Orchestrator must remain strictly neutral until the Final Verdict.
- Don't let the agents devolve into generic AI pleasantries; keep the debate
  sharp and analytical.
- Don't skip the debate steps; the back-and-forth is crucial for deep
  validation.
- In Research Mode, ground novelty judgments in real retrieval whenever
  possible.
- In Research Mode, do not invent optimistic thresholds for data-refuted
  mechanisms.

## Limitations

- The verdict is based on simulated reasoning and logic, not actual market
  data or user feedback.
- This skill is for brainstorming and validation, not a guarantee of business
  success or research acceptance.
- In Research Mode, "not found" in literature search does not prove novelty;
  report it as "no directly overlapping work retrieved under these keywords".

## Conflicts

- **HKUSTDial/Supervisor-Skills** uses a five-dimension framework, fatal-flaws
  audit, and lifecycle/capability matching to produce a reviewer-style verdict.
- **sickn33/agentic-awesome-skills** orchestrates a three-turn Pro/Con debate
  and produces a business-style verdict.
- **HKUSTDial/Supervisor-Skills** requires literature retrieval to ground
  novelty judgments and routes non-STEM ideas to substitute frameworks.
- **sickn33/agentic-awesome-skills** does not use dimension scoring, fatal-flaw
  taxonomies, or literature retrieval; it relies on simulated multi-agent
  debate and logical argumentation.
