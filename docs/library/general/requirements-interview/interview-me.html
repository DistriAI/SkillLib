The copies overlap on the core mechanic (one-question-at-a-time interviewing before building), but they are not interchangeable. The addyosmani and sickn33 copies are near-identical and focus on general software/product intent extraction with a confidence score and 'want vs. should want' probing. The mohitagw15856 copy is a PM-focused variant that adds a specific question sequence (moment of use, success, constraints, prior art, non-goals), a 'forks not open fields' technique, and an assumption ledger. The brycewang-stanford copy is a domain-specific research variant that uses a fixed 6-phase structure (Big Picture, Theory, Data, Identification, Results, Contribution) and outputs a formal Research Specification document. Because the research copy has material steps and a different output format that the others lack, and the PM copy has a different question sequence and ledger, crowning one would hide real alternatives.

# Interview Me

## Overview
Extract the user's actual intent before any plan, spec, or code exists. Use this when a request is underspecified, conventional, or when the user explicitly asks to be interviewed.

## When to Use
- The ask is missing who, why, success criteria, or binding constraints.
- The request is vague ("build me X").
- The user says "interview me" or "grill me".

**Do not use** for unambiguous tasks, mechanical operations, or when you already have ≥95% confidence.

## Process

### 1. Hypothesize
Write a one-sentence hypothesis of what the user wants and a confidence score (0-100%). If below 70%, note what is missing.

### 2. Ask One Question at a Time
Ask a single focused question per turn. Attach a guess to each question to surface your assumptions. Wait for the user's response before proceeding.

**Question Sequencing (General):**
1. **Moment of Use:** Who uses this and what are they doing?
2. **Success:** What does success look like? What would make you send it back?
3. **Constraints:** What are the binding limits (time, cost, format)?
4. **Non-Goals:** What are we explicitly NOT doing?

**Question Sequencing (Research Domain):**
If the task is academic research, use these phases instead:
1. Big Picture (Phenomenon/Puzzle)
2. Theoretical Motivation
3. Data and Setting
4. Identification Strategy
5. Expected Results
6. Contribution

### 3. Probe for Real Intent
Listen for "should want" answers (buzzwords, best-practice deferrals). If heard, ask: "If you didn't have to justify this to anyone, what would you actually want?"

### 4. Restate and Confirm
When confidence is high, restate the intent in 5-8 lines using the user's language. Get explicit confirmation.

**Output Format (General):**
- Outcome: ...
- User: ...
- Why now: ...
- Constraints: ...
- Non-goals: ...

**Output Format (Research):**
Produce a Research Specification Document including Research Question, Motivation, Hypothesis, Empirical Strategy, Data, Expected Results, and Contribution.

### 5. Ledger Assumptions
List any unresolved items as explicit assumptions. Do not silently guess.

- **brycewang-stanford/Auto-Empirical-Research-Skills** uses a fixed research phase structure and specific document output, conflicting with the general flexible process of the other copies.
- **mohitagw15856/pm-claude-skills** prescribes a specific PM question sequence and assumption ledger, which the addyosmani/sickn33 copies omit.
