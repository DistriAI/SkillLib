# Personal Statement

Write a personal statement that is specific, coherent, and unmistakably the applicant's own voice. The statement should show fit and motivation, not a résumé in prose.

## When to Use
- Medical school, residency, fellowship, or graduate school admissions essays.
- University or job application personal statements and statements of purpose.
- Strengthening an existing draft.

## Required Inputs
Ask for these if not provided:
- **The target** — the program/role, institution/company, and any prompt or word limit.
- **What they want** — the field, and what this program/role emphasizes (research potential, clinical experience, specific skills, motivation, etc.).
- **Your material** — experiences, motivations, results, and goals.
- **Your angle** — why you want this and what makes you a fit.
- **Any draft** — existing text to strengthen.

## Framework

### 1. Understand What They Want
Target the statement to what this specific program or role values. Different programs prize different things (research potential, clinical experience, specific skills, motivation). Do not write a one-size essay.

### 2. Find the Through-Line
Identify a single coherent thread — your motivation and the logic of your path to *this* program or role. This turns a list of achievements into a story with direction.

### 3. Show with Evidence
Demonstrate your qualities through specific experiences and results. Replace assertions like "I'm dedicated and hardworking" with proof. For clinical or professional experiences, use the STAR method:
- **S**ituation: Brief context.
- **T**ask: Your responsibility.
- **A**ction: Specific steps you took.
- **R**esult: Measurable outcome plus personal reflection.

### 4. Structure with Purpose
Use a hook-build-close structure. For medical education applications, a standard structure is:
1. **Opening Hook** (10-15%) — A captivating patient story or defining moment.
2. **Clinical Experiences** (30-40%) — Specific patient encounters with reflection.
3. **Research/Academic** (20-25%) — Scholarly contributions and intellectual curiosity.
4. **Service/Leadership** (15-20%) — Community impact and teamwork.
5. **Career Goals** (10-15%) — A clear vision for future practice.

For non-medical applications, adapt the structure to the program's values while keeping the hook-build-close shape.

### 5. Sound Like You
Keep an authentic, genuine voice. Avoid inflation, cliché, and generic openings like "I've always been passionate." Admissions readers see these instantly.

### 6. Stay Honest and On-Brief
Build from real experience within the word limit and prompt. Never invent experiences or results.

## Character Limits (Medical Education)
| Program | Character Limit | Word Approx |
|---------|----------------|-------------|
| AMCAS (Medical School) | 5,300 | ~750 words |
| ERAS (Residency) | Varies by specialty | ~800 words |
| Fellowship | Varies | Varies |

For non-medical applications, use the word limit provided by the target program or role.

## Output Format

### Personal statement: [program/role] · [field] · limit [x]

**What they want:** [program/role values → what to emphasize].
**Your through-line:** [the central motivation/fit thread].
**Structure**
- Opening: [a hook rooted in something real].
- Body: [evidence — experiences/results that show your qualities and path].
- Close: [why you fit + your goals].

**Show, don't assert:** [replace "I'm dedicated" with proof].
**Voice:** [authentic, not inflated].

> Built from *your* real experience — this helps you tell your story, not invent one.

## Quality Checks
- [ ] Targets what the specific program/role values.
- [ ] Has a clear motivation/fit through-line, not a list.
- [ ] Shows qualities with evidence rather than asserting them.
- [ ] Uses a purposeful hook-build-close structure.
- [ ] Keeps an authentic voice, no cliché/inflation.
- [ ] Stays within the prompt/word limit; nothing fabricated.

## Anti-Patterns
- **A résumé in prose** — listing achievements with no thread.
- **Generic "always been passionate"** openings.
- **Asserting qualities** with no evidence.
- **Ignoring what the specific program/role wants.**
- **Inflated voice or fabricated** experiences.
