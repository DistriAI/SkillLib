# Scientific Hypothesis Generation

## 1. Scope and Safety Gate
Before proceeding, confirm authorization for any sensitive or unpublished data. Ensure the work does not cross safety, ethics, or regulatory gates (IRB, biosafety, etc.). Do not present hypotheses as established facts or infer causation from association alone.

## 2. Understand the Phenomenon
- Identify the core observation or anomaly.
- Define scope, boundaries, and constraints.
- Clarify what is known vs. uncertain.

## 3. Literature Search and Synthesis
- Search PubMed (for biomedical) and general web sources.
- Synthesize current understanding, established mechanisms, and gaps.
- Identify contradictory findings or unresolved debates.

## 4. Generate Competing Hypotheses
- Develop 3-5 distinct, mechanistic hypotheses.
- Ensure they are distinguishable and draw on different levels of explanation (molecular, systemic, etc.).

## 5. Evaluate Hypothesis Quality
Assess each hypothesis for:
- Testability and Falsifiability
- Parsimony and Explanatory Power
- Scope and Consistency with established principles
- Novelty

## 6. Design Experimental Tests
- Propose specific experiments (lab, observational, clinical, computational).
- Define measurements, controls, and statistical approaches.
- Address potential confounds.

## 7. Formulate Testable Predictions
- Generate specific, quantitative predictions for each hypothesis.
- Identify conditions under which predictions hold.
- Specify observations that would falsify each hypothesis.

## 8. Report and Visualization
- Produce a structured report including the observation, hypotheses, predictions, and validation plan.
- **Mandatory Visuals:** Include at least 1-2 AI-generated schematics (e.g., hypothesis framework, experimental design flowchart) using the `scientific-schematics` skill or equivalent tool.
- If a LaTeX report is required, use the provided templates and ensure all references are compiled.
